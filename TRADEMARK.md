## A licence to the code is not a licence to the name

The Breathe License and the Apache License both grant rights in **software**.
Neither grants any right to use our names or logos. This is not a trick specific
to us: it is how the Apache License says it in section 6, and how essentially
every open source licence handles trademarks.

So you may fork any Oxy project, run it, modify it and, subject to the licence,
sell it. What you may not do is call the result Oxy, or Mention, or any of the
names above, or use our logos on it.

## What you may do without asking

- Say truthfully that your project uses, integrates with, is compatible with or
  is built on Oxy. Accurate description is not infringement.
- Use the names in articles, talks, reviews, comparisons and criticism,
  including unflattering criticism.
- Use the logos to link to us, unmodified and at their own proportions.
- Say that you contributed to an Oxy project, if you did.

## What needs written permission

- Naming a product, service, company, domain, app or account in a way that could
  be taken for one of ours, including a name that merely contains one of ours.
- Putting our logo on a product, on packaging, or on merchandise.
- Any use that suggests we endorse, sponsor or are affiliated with something,
  when we are not.
- Modifying a logo, including recolouring it, so long as the result still reads
  as ours.

Ask at licensing@oxy.so. We answer.

## Forks

A fork is welcome and the licence protects it. Give it its own name and its own
logo. This is the same rule that Mozilla, WordPress and Linux apply, and it
exists so that a person who downloads something called Oxy knows who wrote it and
who is answerable for it. That is the entire purpose of a trademark, and it is
the one part of this that is genuinely for the user rather than for us.
